Dear Editors,

Please consider “From Bias Scores to Model Rankings: A Measurement Audit of Ten
LLM Probe Families” as a research article.

The manuscript examines the evidence required to use language-model bias audits
in model selection and assurance. It reconstructs a released corpus of 19,500
call records and shows how scenario pairing, response parsing, missingness,
and score definitions change conclusions about stability and cross-probe
agreement. All raw records are preserved, and the revised analysis, sensitivity
specifications, generated figures, and input hashes are supplied for reproduction.

This manuscript revisits our earlier preprint, “Bias Audits Detect Bias but
Disagree on Ranking: Evidence from Ten Instruments and Ten Frontier Models”
(arXiv:2609.15995). The revision materially narrows and changes its interpretation.
It distinguishes adapted probes from the full source benchmarks and does not
claim that every audit detects bias or that meaningful ranking is universally
impossible. Its contribution is a transparent measurement case study with
practical implications for assessing evaluation evidence.

OpenAI Codex, based on GPT-6, assisted with analysis design, code, numerical
reanalysis, literature retrieval, figures, and drafting during preparation on
27 September 2026. The manuscript discloses this assistance. The empirical
results derive from archived response records; no observations or human
annotations were generated to fill missing data.

Sincerely,
William Guey, on behalf of the author group after their approval
